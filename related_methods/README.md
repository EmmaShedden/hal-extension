# Related-work statistical methods on Part 1 (full-data fit and quality of fit)

The main analysis (`R/`, `REPORT.md`) answers the core task with a binomial GLMER. This directory applies the statistical
methods of the related works in `responses.md` to the **same Part 1 data**: 174 paper runs, 172 cells, 21,333 task outcomes,
8 benchmarks. It reports the same quality-of-fit metrics. Part 2 (holdout) was not run for these methods.

* Design, written and committed (`8013967`) before any real-data fit: [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md).
* One subdirectory per method, each with `fit.R`, `results/*.csv` and `fits.Rds` (gitignored, regenerated by `fit.R`).
* Cross-method tables: `comparison_in_sample.csv`, `comparison_run_mae_by_benchmark.csv`.
* Reproduce everything (about 2 minutes): `related_methods/run_all.sh`. Logs go to `logs/related_methods/`.

## 1. The related works' methods

| Dir | Work | Statistical method | Data unit | How it is fit (from the released code) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [`01_rasch_messier`](01_rasch_messier) | Messier (Krsteski et al. 2026) | Rasch / 1PL IRT, `logit P = θ_agent − b_task`, no covariates; 2PL as a robustness check | task-level binary; agent = model + scaffold + effort string | Pyro SVI; hierarchical normal priors with learned SDs (`src/messier/irt.py`). Paper fit keeps the best scaffold per model × benchmark and pools efforts (`analysis/epoch_eci/run.py`) |
| [`02_additive_irt_agent_psychometrics`](02_additive_irt_agent_psychometrics) | Agent Psychometrics (Ge et al. 2026) | 1PL with **additive** ability `θ_LLM + θ_scaffold − b_task`; task difficulty predicted from task features (already E1 / E1b in the main report) | task-level binary, one fit per benchmark | Pyro SVI, hierarchical priors, centred θ's (`swebench_irt/train_model_scaffold_shared.py`); compared against a per-agent py-irt 1PL |
| [`03_benchmark_irt_eci`](03_benchmark_irt_eci) | A Rosetta Stone for AI Benchmarks / ECI (Ho et al. 2025) | aggregate-score IRT `score = σ(α_b (C_m − D_b))` | one score per model × benchmark (max over settings) | least squares + λ = 0.1 ridge, bounds, anchor benchmark (`fit.py`, `data_loader.py`) |
| [`04_lowrank_benchpress`](04_lowrank_benchpress) | BenchPress (Zeng & Papailiopoulos 2026) | low-rank matrix completion in logit space, `Z = μ + a_m + c_b + U_m V_bᵀ` | model × benchmark score matrix | ridge ALS, rank 2, λ = 0.1, 10-seed ensemble (`methods/completers.py: complete_bias_als`) |
| [`05_collab_filtering_cpp`](05_collab_filtering_cpp) | Collaborative Performance Prediction (Zhang et al. 2024) | collaborative filtering: MF `⟨p_m, q_t⟩`, and a factor-enhanced neural CF that adds model factors (family, size, ...) and task factors (ability, ...) | model × task score matrix, min-max normalised per model | SGD, d = 10, lr 0.01, 250k epochs (`src/mf.py`, `src/ncf_f.py`) |
| [`06_skill_scaling_sloth`](06_skill_scaling_sloth) | Sloth (Maia Polo et al. 2024/25) | low-dimensional skills that are a **function of model covariates** (log params, log tokens) with family-specific intercepts, mapped to benchmarks by a sigmoid | model × benchmark scores | SmoothL1 loss, full-batch Adam (`sloth/sloth.py`) |
| [`07_observational_scaling`](07_observational_scaling) | Observational Scaling Laws (Ruan et al. 2024) | PCA "capabilities" from cheap non-agentic benchmarks; agentic scores regressed on PC1–3 through a sigmoid with a floor | model × benchmark scores | PCA imputation + sigmoid least squares (`utils/data.py`) |

All methods in `responses.md`'s seven "closest existing studies" with a statistical model to fit are covered.
* **Efficient Benchmarking** selects tasks rather than fitting a model.
* The rest of `responses.md`'s list is either within-benchmark IRT (whose 1PL/2PL core is method 01), needs inputs HAL does not have (parameter counts, lineage graphs, trajectories, human times), or is about measurement validity rather than prediction.
* DESIGN.md "Not applied" gives the reason per work.

**Adaptations forced by HAL** (all in DESIGN.md, set before fitting):
* **Effort.** HAL varies reasoning effort, which the aggregate methods fold into the model row (model × effort, 21 rows), as ECI does.
* **Scaffold.** HAL varies scaffold, which the aggregate methods treat as part of the measurement (scaffold × benchmark, 15 columns).
* **Sloth.** It needs parameter and token counts, which are unknown for 13 of 15 models. Release date stands in as the scale proxy.
* **OSL.** Its original predictors (MMLU, GSM8K, ...) are not reported for these models. Seven Epoch benchmarks that cover ≥ 12 of 15 models are used instead.
* **CPP.** Its neural factor model needs PyTorch. The factor-embedding idea is kept inside plain MF.
* **Code over paper text.** Where a paper's text and code disagree (Messier's priors, BenchPress's biases), the code is followed.

### Reading the table
1. **In-sample fit ranks flexibility, not interpolation skill.** The ordering follows how many free parameters each run or cell gets.
   * CPP has 360–610 parameters for 172 cells and interpolates every cell (0.03 pp).
   * AP's per-agent, per-benchmark 1PL gives each cell its own ability and reaches 0.92 pp.
   * The GLMER's run effect does the same job (1.28 pp).
   * None of these numbers says how well a *missing* combination would be predicted. That is Part 2's question, and these methods have not been through it.
2. **Task-level methods.** Among methods with task information (01, 02), the GLMER's conditional fit is matched, not beaten, on the task metrics.
   * Messier's 2PL has lower log-loss (0.329 vs 0.353) and higher AUC (0.919 vs 0.910). It spends 1,084 extra discrimination parameters to get there.
   * Its 1PL is about level with the GLMER (0.357 / 0.907).
   * What the GLMER adds is run-level accuracy (1.3 vs 2.7–3.7 pp) through its run and interaction effects, plus interpretable covariates.
3. **Messier's capability-scale fit is a poor *fit* to HAL by design.** It keeps one θ per model across all 8 benchmarks with one scaffold each, which gives 9.3 pp run MAE (ECI's faithful variant, also one capability per model, gives 5.8 pp on its maxima).
   * Model strength in HAL is strongly benchmark-specific, as the GLMER's model × benchmark term (SD 0.40) and the ECI capability ranking both show.
   * In ECI's ranking, Claude Opus 4.1 sits above GPT-5, and o3 is level with GPT-4.1 (−1.13 vs −1.11). This is unlike Epoch's own ECI, where GPT-5 > o3 > Opus 4.1 (`03_benchmark_irt_eci/results/capability.csv`).
4. **Agent Psychometrics' additivity holds less well on HAL than on SWE-bench leaderboards.**
   * The additive model's AUC is 0.006 lower than the per-agent model's (`additive_vs_per_agent_auc.csv`). The gap is −0.015 on SWE-bench and −0.011 on GAIA.
   * AP reported a mean |ΔAUC| of about 0.002.
   * This agrees with the GLMER's model × scaffold variance (SD 0.44 logits, LRT p = 1e-4): the scaffold's effect depends on the model.
5. **Covariate-only methods (06, 07) fit run accuracy far better than the GLMER's fixed part (3–8 vs 12.4 pp), but not for the reason it might seem.**
   * They give every column its own loading on the covariates: 41–93 parameters for 172 cells. The GLMER fixed part has 20 coefficients and puts benchmark-specific model strength in its random terms.
   * OSL shows how fragile this is. Its per-column sigmoid is unregularised as in the original, and on `HAL Generalist Agent|CORE-Bench Hard` (15 cells) it is near-separated, with PC coefficients up to 683 (`07_observational_scaling/results/column_fits.csv`).
   * These fits need the Part 2 holdout before they say anything about prediction. Sloth's 3-skill fit is the most promising covariate-only candidate, at 3.0 pp in-sample.
6. **External capability measures carry real signal.** OSL with PC1–3 of seven non-agentic benchmarks (PC1 = 75 % of variance) fits as well as ECI fit *on HAL itself* (5.0 vs 5.1 pp).
   * Epoch's ECI used as the capability gives 7.7 pp, with a positive slope on all 15 columns.
   * This is the only route here that could predict a brand-new model from public numbers. The GLMER's new-model error is 10.7 pp out of time (REPORT.md §4).
7. **Low-rank completion (BenchPress) is the most economical aggregate fit.** At 109 parameters it reaches 3.1 pp, better than ECI's 1-D σ(α(C − D)) at 5.1 pp. One sigmoid capability does not describe the 15 HAL columns; a second dimension helps.

### Caveats
* **In-sample only.** Every number above is the fit to the data the method was fit on. Part 2 holdouts are the comparison that matters for the interpolation question, and they were not run here.
* **Aggregate vs task-level.** Methods 03–07 never see task-level outcomes. Their task log-loss and AUC are therefore near the GLMER's fixed-only values and are not comparable to methods 01–02.
* **Approximations to the papers' fitting procedures** (DESIGN.md):
  * The Messier and AP fits use the Laplace GLMM in place of SVI with learned prior SDs. These are the same hierarchical model.
  * The 2PL is MAP with the 1PL's variance estimates.
  * CPP's neural towers are reduced to factor embeddings in MF.
  * Sloth and OSL use substitute covariates or benchmarks.
* **Singular fits.** One of AP's per-benchmark additive fits is singular: SciCode's scaffold SD is 0, with two scaffolds.

## 3. Post-fit changes
None to the design. Three scripts (03, 05, 06) crashed in their reporting code after fitting, from a data.table name clash and an `rbind` call. The reporting code was fixed and the scripts rerun with identical seeds; nothing in the fits changed (DESIGN.md "Post-fit changes").
